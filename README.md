# The Wage Trajectory Ledger
An open-source macroeconomic calculator tracking the 20-year stagnation of UK working-class real wages, cost-of-living decoupling, and stealth fiscal drag threshold freezes.

## 📢 1. THE DECEPTION OF HISTORICAL STAGNATION
For more than two decades, working-class households across the United Kingdom have been subjected to an aggressive, systemic erosion of their actual purchasing power. While nominal wages appear to rise incrementally on paper, the true weight of the pound against core real-world liabilities—including residential housing, domestic energy utility costs, transport, and essential foodstuffs—has thoroughly decoupled from earnings.

Following the 2008 banking sector bailout and subsequent state-enforced austerity measures, real-wage growth underwent a historic structural stagnation. When administrative agencies broadcast narrow annual consumer inflation windows (such as a temporary 2% or 5% baseline), they systematically obscure the multi-decade compounding reality: a structural loss of roughly 30% in lifetime worker wage trajectories.

This repository serves as a purely transparent, open-source mathematical auditor. It strips away political PR spin by comparing modern nominal incomes directly against the historical cost-of-living purchasing metrics since the turn of the millennium (the year 2000).

## 🔒 2. THE STEALTH TAX ENGINE: FISCAL DRAG EXTENDED TO 2030
The primary administrative mechanism used to quietly extract wealth from working-class earnings without explicitly raising headline tax rates is known as **Fiscal Drag**. 

By freezing the tax-free Personal Allowance threshold at £12,570 while nominal wages face minor inflationary pressure, the state systematically drags lower-income earners into higher tax bands. Following recent state directives, these critical threshold locks have been formally extended all the way to **April 2030**. 

If the baseline personal tax thresholds from the year 2000 were simply indexed to match the real inflation of core domestic assets and living costs, the tax-free personal allowance today would be significantly higher, saving the average working-class person thousands of pounds in annual stealth taxation.
